Structure of Chromosome & DNA

Chromosome Organization

A chromosome is made of DNA and proteins. The long thread of DNA wraps around histone proteins to form nucleosomes, which then coil up to form chromatin thread. Chromatin further condenses to form chromosomes.

Significance of this Organization:

  • Allows the long chain of DNA to be packed densely.
  • DNA molecules are protected by the proteins.
  • Allows accurate sorting of chromosomes during cell division without tangling.

DNA Structure Details

  • Double helix structure, with two sugar-phosphate chains running in opposite directions (antiparallel).
  • Pairs of bases form horizontal connections between the chains.
  • Dimensions: 2 nm width, 3.4 nm per complete turn, 0.34 nm between adjacent bases.

Worked Example 1: Base Composition

If a sample of DNA has 18% guanine (G), what percentage is thymine (T)?

Answer: G pairs with C, so C = 18%. Total G + C = 36%. Remaining A + T = 64%. Since A = T, T = 32%.

The Genetic Code & Protein Synthesis

Key Properties of the Genetic Code

  • Triplet Code: Three consecutive bases (a codon) specify one particular amino acid.
  • Start Codon: AUG codes for Methionine (Met) and starts protein synthesis.
  • Degenerate Code: A single amino acid may be coded by more than one triplet. (4³ = 64 combinations for 20 amino acids).
  • Non-overlapping: Bases are assigned to form a codon and will not be shared with other bases to form another codon.
  • Universal: The same triplet code works for the same amino acids in all organisms.
  • Nonsense Triplets: Do not code for any known amino acid; serve as stop codons (e.g., UAA, UAG, UGA).

Recombinant DNA Technology

Core Steps in Genetic Engineering

Isolate desired gene and cut plasmid

  • Use a restriction enzyme to isolate the desired gene from the source.
  • Use the same restriction enzyme to cut the plasmid.
  • This creates complementary sticky ends on both the target gene and the plasmid.

Join the DNA

  • Use DNA ligase to join the desired gene and the cut plasmid together via complementary base pairing between the unpaired bases in their sticky ends.
  • Result: Recombinant plasmid.

Introduce into host cells

  • Introduce the recombinant plasmids into bacterial cells.
  • Methods: Treatment with ice-cold calcium chloride (CaCl₂) followed by brief incubation at 40°C, or brief electric shock.

Selection & Mass Culture

  • Select transformed bacteria by culturing on nutrient agar containing a specific antibiotic (only bacteria with the plasmid survive).
  • Mass culture the transformed bacterial cells in a nutrient medium containing the antibiotic.
  • Induce the expression of the gene to produce the protein product.
  • Extract and purify the gene product (e.g., human insulin).

Application: Human Insulin Production

  • Mass produced to meet increasing demand instead of using pig insulin.
  • Advantages: Eliminates transmission of animal pathogens, eliminates allergens from pig insulin, and eliminates immune responses since the amino acid sequence matches human insulin.

Concepts from Past Papers

  • Plasmid as a vector: Carries foreign genes and transfers them to host cells.
  • Antibiotic resistance: Used as a selectable marker. If a target gene is inserted into the middle of a tetracycline resistance gene, that gene is interrupted (no longer functional), so the bacteria lose tetracycline resistance.

PCR Curve Analysis

  • Exponential Growth: DNA doubles every cycle (2^n copies after n cycles).
  • Plateau (Levelling out): Occurs because nucleotides and primers get used up, and polymerase loses activity.

DNA Fingerprinting & Gel Electrophoresis

Principles: VNTRs & RFLP

  • VNTRs (Variable Number Tandem Repeats): Short sequences of repeated DNA in non-coding regions. The number of repeats varies greatly between individuals. Inherited one allele from each parent.
  • RFLP (Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism): Restriction enzymes break DNA into fragments of different lengths. This polymorphism is used in DNA fingerprinting.

Applications

  • Medical Diagnostics: Screening for inherited diseases or cancer mutations.
  • Food Composition: Detecting GM foods or authenticating natural products.
  • Archaeology: Studying ancient DNA and human migration patterns.

Gene Therapy & Stem Cell Therapy

Gene Therapy

Treatment of genetic diseases by substituting a defective gene with a therapeutic (normal) gene.

In Vivo Gene Therapy

  • Therapeutic gene is inserted into a vector (e.g., virus).
  • The viral vector is introduced directly into the patient's body.
  • The gene is inserted into target cells (e.g., liver cells).

Ex Vivo Gene Therapy

  • Therapeutic gene is inserted into a vector.
  • Target cells are removed from the patient's body and grown in culture.
  • Viruses are added to insert the gene into the cells.
  • The modified cells are transplanted back into the patient.

Examples & Vectors:

  • SCID (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency): Caused by a mutation preventing lymphocyte production. Bone marrow stem cells are targeted ex vivo using a viral vector carrying the ADA gene.
  • Cystic Fibrosis: Defective CFTR gene (chloride channel). In vivo therapy using liposome-based vectors (safe and can target specific cells, unlike viruses) delivered via aerosol to the lungs.

Hazards:

  • Random insertion into the genome may cause harmful mutations or trigger cancer (leukemia).
  • Immune response against the vector.
  • Viral vector may regain ability to cause disease.

Stem Cell Therapy

Stem cells are unspecialized cells capable of dividing indefinitely (mitosis) and differentiating into specialized cells.

Feature Adult Stem Cells Embryonic Stem Cells
Immune Rejection No rejection (derived from patient) May trigger immune response
Supply Limited Actively dividing, unlimited
Ethical Issues Less political/ethical issues More ethical issues (destruction of embryos)
Differentiation Limited cell types Can differentiate into almost all cell types

Applications:

  • COVID-19: Umbilical cord blood stem cells are cultured, differentiated into epithelial stem cells, and transferred to lungs to regenerate damaged epithelium.
  • Diabetes: Differentiate into pancreatic cells to treat insulin-dependent diabetes.

Transgenic Organisms & Cloning

Transgenic Plants & Animals

  • GM Salmon: Additional growth hormone gene -> faster growth/larger size.
  • BT Crops: Produce BT toxin to kill pests.
  • Golden Rice: Produces precursor of vitamin A (carotene) to reduce night blindness.
  • Agrobacterium: A soil bacterium used to transfer genes to plants. It contains a Ti plasmid that can integrate foreign DNA into the plant genome.
  • Gene Gun: Fires DNA-coated gold particles at high velocity into plant/animal tissue. (Advantage: Direct, no plasmid needed. Disadvantage: Random insertion, mechanical damage, costly).

Animal Cloning (e.g., Dolly the Sheep)

Process: Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer (SCNT)

  1. Remove nucleus from an unfertilized egg cell.
  2. Fuse the enucleated egg cell with a somatic cell (e.g., udder cell) from the donor using electricity.
  3. Implant the resulting embryo into a surrogate mother.
  4. The clone is genetically identical to the donor of the somatic cell.

Plant Cloning (Tissue Culture)

  1. Remove meristematic cells from a parent plant.
  2. Place in sterile culture medium with nutrients.
  3. Cells grow by mitosis to form a mass of undifferentiated cells called a callus.
  4. Transfer callus to a medium with plant hormones (auxins/cytokinins) to stimulate root and shoot growth.
  5. Transfer plantlets to soil.

Monoclonal Antibodies

Production (Hybridoma Technology)

  1. Inject a specific antigen into a mouse to stimulate B cells.
  2. Extract B cells (short-lived) from the spleen.
  3. Fuse B cells with myeloma (tumour) cells (long-lived) to form hybridomas.
  4. Screen and culture the desired hybridoma. They will continuously secrete a large amount of identical antibodies (monoclonal antibodies).

Applications

  • Pregnancy Test: Monoclonal antibodies bind to hCG hormone in urine. Binding causes a colour change on the test strip.
  • Cancer Treatment: Trastuzumab binds to HER-2 receptors on breast cancer cells, blocking hormones from triggering cell division. Also used to carry radioactive substances/toxins to cancer cells specifically.
  • Organ Transplant: Prevent rejection by attacking T cells.
  • COVID-19: Bind to the spike protein of the virus to block entry into cells.

Bioethics

Ethical, Legal, Social, Economic, and Environmental Issues

  • GM foods may contain foreign genes forbidden by some religions.
  • Long-term effects on human health are unknown.
  • Antibiotic-resistance marker genes may be transferred to pathogens ("superbugs").
  • Gene pollution: Pollen from GM crops may cross-pollinate wild plants, upsetting ecological balance.
  • Wastage of embryos during cloning.
  • Destroys the naturally developed 'biparent' society (uniparent trend).
  • Confusion in family relationships.
  • Human cloning is illegal and raises eugenics concerns.
  • Germ-line gene therapy causes irreversible changes to future generations.
  • Who has the right to choose to change genes (e.g., IQ, skin color)?
  • Violation of nature / playing God.
  • Who owns personal genetic information? (Privacy vs Insurance companies).
  • Psychological impact of knowing undesirable genetic traits.
